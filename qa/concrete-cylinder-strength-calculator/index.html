How the strength is calculated

Compressive strength = maximum load ÷ cross-sectional area, with area = π × d² ÷ 4. With load in newtons and area in mm² the result is in MPa (N/mm²); psi = MPa × 145.0377 (exact factors: 1 lbf = 4.4482216152605 N, 1 in = 25.4 mm). Two checks: 250 kN on a 100 mm cylinder is 31.83 MPa, and 100,000 lbf on a 4 in cylinder is 7,958 psi.

Use the measured diameter where your test method asks for it (it may be the average of two measurements at right angles); the calculator uses whatever diameter you enter. If you enter the length, it shows L/D. Your test method sets whether a short specimen (low L/D) needs a strength correction and what the factor is; enter that factor and every result is multiplied by it. Leave it empty for no correction.

The calculator does not decide acceptance. How many cylinders make a test, whether the result is one cylinder or the average of a set, and the acceptance rules come from your project specification and the code it follows. The PASS/FAIL column only compares each cylinder with the required strength you enter.
