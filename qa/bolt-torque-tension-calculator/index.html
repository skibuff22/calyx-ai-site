The formula

T = K × F × d, with T the tightening torque, F the bolt tension (preload), d the nominal diameter and K the nut factor, a single number that lumps together thread friction, friction under the nut and thread geometry. The tool works in N, m and N·m internally; unit factors are exact (1 lbf = 4.4482216152605 N, 1 in = 0.0254 m, 1 ft·lbf = 1.3558179483314 N·m). Check: d = 12 mm, K = 0.2, F = 20 kN gives T = 0.2 × 20,000 N × 0.012 m = 48 N·m, and 48 N·m back to tension gives 20 kN. Results show tension to 0.001 kN and 1 lbf, torque to 0.1 N·m, 0.1 ft·lbf and 1 in·lbf, K to 0.001.

The tool does not choose K. It changes with lubrication, plating, thread condition and washer, so take it from the fastener or anchor manufacturer, your spec, or your own torque-tension test, and treat the result as an estimate: the same torque can give a quite different tension when friction changes. Where the tension itself matters, measure it rather than infer it from torque. This is not a design tool and it does not set an installation torque; that comes from the anchor manufacturer's instructions or your spec. To convert a torque from a data sheet, use the installation torque converter; to turn the tension into stress, the bolt tensile stress area calculator; to read a load cell in mV/V, the load cell mV/V to load calculator.
